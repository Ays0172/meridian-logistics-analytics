# Day 6 — Solutions

## Spaced recall

1. `USERELATIONSHIP` changes which relationship a specific measure's calculation uses, but has no effect on which relationship a slicer or visual-level filter travels through; importing a dimension multiple times gives each role its own always-active relationship, so slicers built from each imported copy filter independently and simultaneously.
2. `DimCustomer` relates directly to both `FactBooking` and `FactShipment`, and `FactBooking` relates directly to `FactShipment` via `BookingKey` — a triangle. It's only dangerous once one edge of that triangle (typically `DimCustomer`–`FactBooking`) is made bidirectional, because that opens a second route by which a filter on `FactShipment` could reach `DimCustomer`, creating an ambiguous cycle; with every edge left single-direction, the triangle is harmless.
3. A dashed line tells you the relationship is inactive (propagates no filter unless activated via `USERELATIONSHIP` in a specific measure). It tells you nothing about cardinality — the `1`/`*` markers at each end are an entirely separate visual signal, and a dashed one-to-many relationship looks structurally the same shape as a solid one at a glance.
4. Per the `-1` Unknown-member convention, an unresolved `AtaDateKey` still participates in the relationship and shows up as an explicit "Unknown" bucket; a null `AtaDateKey` would fall outside any filter built on `DimDate` and the in-transit shipment would simply disappear from totals filtered through that role, rather than appearing as an honest, countable "Unknown."
5. Use `USERELATIONSHIP` when only one role needs to be active in a given calculation at a time and you never need two roles slicer-filterable simultaneously on the same visual (the common case for date roles); import the dimension multiple times when two or more roles must be independently, simultaneously filterable (the common case for the four location roles).
6. "Assume referential integrity" makes the relationship generate an inner-join-style query instead of an outer join, which is faster — but it's only safe once every fact-side FK has been correctly routed to a real dimension key (including `-1` for unresolved cases) with no genuine orphans, since the setting can otherwise silently drop rows that should have matched the Unknown member.

## Drill 1 — Fiscal year and ISO week, by hand

Using the formula `FiscalMonth = ((CalendarMonth − 10) mod 12) + 1` and the "fiscal year named for the calendar year it ends in" convention:

**(a) 15 November 2025** — calendar month 11: `((11−10) mod 12)+1 = 2`. Fiscal month 2 falls in **FQ1** (fiscal months 1–3). The fiscal year covering October 2025–September 2026 ends in 2026, so **FiscalYear = 2026 (FY26)**.

**(b) 3 October 2026** — calendar month 10: `((10−10) mod 12)+1 = 1`. Fiscal month 1 is the first month of **FQ1**. October 2026 starts the fiscal year running through September 2027, so **FiscalYear = 2027 (FY27)** — note this is a full fiscal year later than (a), despite the calendar dates being less than a year apart, because 3 October 2026 has just crossed the 1 October fiscal-year boundary.

**(c) 31 December 2023** — calendar month 12: `((12−10) mod 12)+1 = 3`. Fiscal month 3 is the last month of **FQ1**, and the fiscal year running October 2023–September 2024 ends in 2024, so **FiscalYear = 2024 (FY24)**.

**ISO week/year for (c), mechanism shown:** 31 December 2023 is a Sunday, so the ISO week containing it runs Monday 25 December to Sunday 31 December 2023. That week's Thursday is 28 December 2023 — inside calendar year 2023 — so by the "a week belongs to the year containing its Thursday" rule, this week is part of **ISO year 2023**, not 2024, even though it's the very last week of the year. Counting forward from ISO week 1 of 2023 (which starts Monday 2 January 2023, since 1 January 2023 was a Sunday and the first Thursday of January was 5 January) to the week of 25–31 December is exactly 51 full weeks later, making this **ISO week 52 of ISO year 2023**.

## Drill 2 — The Auto Date/Time trap

With Auto Date/Time on and no date table marked, a `TOTALYTD` measure built against a date field dragged straight from a fact table resets its "year-to-date" boundary at **1 January**, not 1 October — because the hidden, auto-generated calendar behind that field uses the standard Gregorian calendar year, with no awareness of Meridian's fiscal year at all. Concretely, a YTD total evaluated in, say, November 2025 under the auto-generated hierarchy accumulates from 1 January 2025 (10 months of activity), while the *intended* fiscal YTD — once `DimDate` is properly marked and the visual is rebuilt using a fiscal-aware measure against `DimDate` — should accumulate from 1 October 2025 (about 1–2 months of activity, depending on the exact date). The two numbers will differ substantially, and the discrepancy is exactly the size of the extra nine calendar months the auto-generated calendar wrongly included. If your own test showed the numbers matching, double check that Auto Date/Time was genuinely still switched on and that you dragged the field from the fact table rather than from `DimDate` — the trap only manifests under that exact combination.

## Drill 3 — Both account-manager measures

A correct build has, on every `DimCustomer` row (current and historical alike), two account-manager columns: the row's own `AccountManager` and the `LOOKUPVALUE`-derived `CurrentAccountManager`. Any `CustomerCode` that has gone through an account-manager change (one of the four SCD2 trigger attributes) will show a real, visible difference between `Revenue by AM at Time` (grouped by `AccountManager`) and `Revenue by Current AM` (grouped by `CurrentAccountManager`) for the two names involved: the departing AM's "at time" total will include revenue from before the handover that the "current AM" view instead attributes entirely to the successor. For example, if account manager "R. Iyer" managed a customer until a reassignment to "S. Menon" partway through the period, `Revenue by AM at Time` correctly splits that customer's revenue between both names according to who actually owned the account when each shipment happened, while `Revenue by Current AM` attributes the *entire* customer's revenue — including the pre-handover portion — to S. Menon alone. Neither total is "wrong"; they're answering different, equally legitimate questions, and the size of the gap is a direct, visible measure of how much business happened before versus after the handover.

## Drill 4 — The `IsCurrent` trap, walked into

Applying `DimCustomer[IsCurrent] = 1` as a page filter produces a **lower** total revenue figure than the unfiltered total. The mechanism, stated precisely: every fact row whose `CustomerKey` points at a *non-current* (historical) `DimCustomer` version — i.e., every transaction that happened before that customer's most recent SCD2 change — no longer has a matching row in the filtered dimension, so the single-direction relationship excludes those fact rows from the visual entirely. This is not "some numbers rounded differently" — it's specific fact rows disappearing from the total because their dimension-side match was filtered away, and the size of the drop is, roughly, the total historical revenue from every customer who has had at least one SCD2 change, restricted to the period before their most recent change. Removing the filter restores the full total because every fact row, historical or current, is once again matched against some `DimCustomer` row.

## Drill 5 — The frozen "today"

`TODAY()` in a live measure recalculates on every refresh to the actual current date, moving forward every single day; `DimDate.IsCurrentYear` was computed once, at generation time, relative to a fixed anchor (2026-08-31), and never moves. The moment real time passes that anchor, any live measure comparing dates against `TODAY()` will disagree with `IsCurrentYear`'s frozen assessment of which year counts as "current." The one-line fix, if the two need to agree indefinitely: stop relying on the stored flag for anything that must stay live, and compute "current year" directly in the measure itself — e.g. `FILTER(DimDate, DimDate[Year] = YEAR(TODAY()))` — rather than filtering on the pre-computed `IsCurrentYear` column, accepting the minor extra calculation cost in exchange for a definition of "current" that never goes stale.
